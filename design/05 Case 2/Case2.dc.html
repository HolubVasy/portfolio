Case 02 · IT services, internal employee portal · 2025

140+ endpoints moved from in-memory caches to the database

Company  IT services company, under NDARole  .NET developer, de facto lead of the .NET part

Moved 140+ portal endpoints from static in-memory caches to direct database queries. Every endpoint was checked against the old version, and all but 2 or 3 of them answer within 1 second.

Challenge

The portal kept almost everything it knew about employees in static in-memory caches. As the data grew wider (projects, roles, technologies), performance dropped and cached data could go missing. Two requirements came from the director. No endpoint may become slower, and none may take longer than 1 second.

Constraints

The change cut through the whole project while other features were being built in parallel. Resolving merge conflicts took 2 to 3 hours every day. There were no tests and no business description of a correct response, so the old endpoint became the specification.

Decisions
01
Remove the cache instead of keeping it

Instead of extending the cache, which did not fit the company's policy. Nobody could say how fresh the cached data was, and the cache was heavy to build and hold.

02
Two environments, old and new, compared by script

The same request went to both, and Postman post-request scripts compared the responses over mock JSON data covering every entity.

Results
All but 2 or 3 endpoints
answer within the 1-second target, out of 140+
Fewer bugs
no more data disappearing from the cache, and fresher data in the portal
Prod-like environment
built from a sanitized copy of production after the first release was rolled back
Takeaway

A large cross-cutting change belongs in one branch and gets tested in an environment that looks like production. It only works if the rest of the team knows about it and works with you.
